# Facial Recognition at Airline Boarding Gates: Phase 2 Literature Survey and State of the Art (as of 29 Sep 2026)

**Input:** Phase 1 handoff (authoritative) and `phase1-problem.md`. Problems covered: the top five (P13, P1, P14, P15, P10), plus P4 (quality), P6 (PAD), P7 (demographics), P2/P16 (walk-through, multi-face, tailgating) and P19 (secure templates), because they are likely POC components or directly shape P1 and P10.

**Label legend:** [V] = VERIFIED (a source states it). [I] = INFERRED (my reasoning). ⚠️OLD = source older than about 24 months (before about Sep 2024) and used to describe the current state. Foundational methods (such as ArcFace) are flagged ⚠️OLD but remain the standard baselines.

**GPU assumption:** The project context leaves the GPU model and VRAM as placeholders. "Feasible on my GPU = Y" below means inference, evaluation and light fine-tuning on one consumer NVIDIA GPU with at least 8 GB VRAM. It does not mean training a recognizer from scratch on WebFace-scale data. [I]

**Phase 1 check:** No errors found in Phase 1. One update: the US rule letting DHS photograph all non-citizens on entry and exit took effect on 26 Dec 2025, so the CBP programme status is now known. Whether the airline still uses CBP biometric boarding on US flights is still not publicly known. [V] https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/27/2025-19655/collection-of-biometric-data-from-aliens-upon-entry-to-and-departure-from-the-united-states (27 Oct 2025)

## TL;DR
- **Matching accuracy is close to solved on cooperative images; the gate problems are now in the system around it.** An analysis of NIST FRTE 1:1 results finds leading algorithms "converging on mature verification benchmarks," with visa-to-border FNMR at 0.14% for the leaders. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202608/nist-frte-11-shows-facial-verification-accuracy-converging (11 Aug 2026). In the DHS 2022 walk-through rally, acquisition systems caused 97% of errors. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202302/dhs-biometric-rally-results-show-groups-pose-little-challenge-to-effective-matching (23 Feb 2023) ⚠️OLD
- **Gallery scoping matters.** Median 1:N miss rate across NIST FRTE algorithms rises from 0.61% at 640k identities to 10.34% at 12M (FPIR 0.003). Flight-manifest galleries, as CBP's TVS uses, keep search small. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202609/new-nist-frte-1n-results-illustrate-facial-recognition-accuracys-multidimensionality (16 Sep 2026)
- **Quality scores are for capture feedback, not rejection.** DHS found OFIQ's unified score of "extremely limited utility," and a UNHCR evaluation found about 90% of images that a UQS threshold would reject were still usable. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202609/better-face-recognition-exposes-limits-of-image-quality-scores (24 Sep 2026)
- **Template protection is becoming practical.** Homomorphic-encrypted search over 1M templates took 126 ms (about 2× plaintext) in ICCV 2025 work. Diffusion-based attacks can rebuild usable faces from embeddings, so templates should be treated as biometric data. [V] https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Kim_IDFace_Face_Template_Protection_for_Efficient_and_Secure_Identification_ICCV_2025_paper.html (2025); https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13168 (Feb 2026)
- **Integration, resilience and operations (P13–P15) are shaped by industry architectures, not papers.** CBP TVS (government-built galleries matched in the cloud, with the airline as camera operator) is the closest public analogue to the airline matching against the identity authority. [I] The 2026 EU EES rollout showed that even national systems need a planned degraded mode. [V] https://time.com/article/2026/08/12/eu-europe-travel-entry-exit-system-ees-passport-biometric/ (12 Aug 2026)

## A. State of the art per problem

**Recognition core (feeds P1, P2, P18).** Margin-based softmax losses on normalized embeddings are still the backbone of the field: ArcFace (CVPR 2019) ⚠️OLD, MagFace (CVPR 2021) ⚠️OLD and AdaFace (CVPR 2022) ⚠️OLD. AdaFace R100 trained on WebFace12M reports IJB-C TAR 97.66% at FAR 1e-4. [V] https://github.com/mk-minchul/AdaFace (repo, accessed 29 Sep 2026). Newer academic work improves on these margins mainly through backbones and training strategy rather than new losses. [I] Examples are TopoFR (NeurIPS 2024, topology alignment) [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10587 (Oct 2024), KP-RPE (CVPR 2024, landmark-aware ViT position encoding, aimed at low-quality faces) [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14852 (Mar 2024) ⚠️OLD, and LVFace (ICCV 2025 highlight). LVFace ranked first in the MFR-Ongoing academic track (Dec 2024) and reports IJB-C 97.70% at 1e-4 but only 90.06% at 1e-6. [V] https://huggingface.co/bytedance-research/LVFace (2025). A 2025 survey names scalable training, synthetic data and interpretability as the open problems. [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24247 (Kim, Jain, Liu; May 2025, rev. Dec 2025). Synthetic-identity fine-tuning of foundation models (CLIP ViT-L/14) was the IJCB 2026 competition theme, but is not shown to reach real-data parity. [V] https://idtechwire.com/ijcb-competition-shows-gains-from-synthetic-face-recognition-training-data/ (29 Jul 2026). For edge hardware, EdgeFace (1.77M parameters) reports IJB-C 94.85% [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01838 (TBIOM 2024) ⚠️OLD, and InsightFace's MobileFaceNet pack reports 95.02% against 97.25% for its R50 pack. That is a gap of roughly 2–3 points at 1e-4, which widens at the very low FMRs gates need. [V] https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/model_zoo/README.md (accessed 29 Sep 2026); [I] widening. Operational leadership is measured by NIST FRTE, where only vendors compete (see C).

**P13 Integration.** There is no academic literature on keeping app, loyalty, DCS, government identity and gate events consistent. The state of the art is industry architecture plus distributed-systems patterns. [I] IATA One ID defines Recommended Practices 1701o (contactless travel), 1701p (digitalisation of admissibility) and 1701q (biometric handling), built on consent, decentralised identity and interoperability. It published a position paper on "Contactless Travel at Scale" in April 2026 and counts 50+ airlines, 40+ airports and 25+ government entities engaged. [V] https://www.iata.org/en/programs/passenger/one-id/ (2026). CBP TVS is the most documented government-anchored model: CBP builds galleries from advance passenger manifests using passport, visa and prior-encounter photos, and matches in its cloud. Airline and airport partners operate the cameras, may not retain photos and must purge them after transmission. [V] https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/27/2025-19655/collection-of-biometric-data-from-aliens-upon-entry-to-and-departure-from-the-united-states (27 Oct 2025). The airline matching against identity-authority profiles (Phase 1) is structurally similar, but the airline's integration contract with the identity authority is not publicly known. [I] Decentralised alternatives exist. Digi Yatra (India) stores verifiable credentials on the passenger's phone and has 21M active users across 24 airports. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202602/planned-digi-yatra-feature-to-enable-credential-sharing-for-global-expansion (16 Feb 2026). ICAO DTC Type 1 is piloted (Finland: under 8 s against 25 s at kiosks), while Types 2 and 3 are "not here yet." [V] https://identityweek.net/icao-dtc-could-change-travel-rulebook-but-dtc-types-2-and-3-eliminating-passports-not-here-yet/ (14 Apr 2025). Engineering patterns that apply: the transactional outbox, so that "passenger boarded" is written and published atomically [V] https://microservices.io/patterns/data/transactional-outbox.html (undated), and idempotency keys for retried gate events. The IETF Idempotency-Key header draft reached -07 on 15 Oct 2025 and expired on 18 Apr 2026, so it is not a finished standard. [V] https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header/history/ (accessed 29 Sep 2026)

**P1 1:N thresholds and flight galleries.** Gate matching is open-set identification: the system must decide whether the probe is in the gallery and who it is, measured as FNIR at a fixed FPIR. [V] NIST's paperless-travel protocol simulates 420-person boarding galleries with thresholds set at FPIR 0.0003, and allows k enrolment images per person. [V] https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt_paperless_travel.html (leaderboard updated 28 Mar 2024) ⚠️OLD. Gallery scoping is the strongest lever. The Sept 2026 FRTE 1:N update shows median FNIR growing from 0.61% (640k) to 1.24% (3M) and 10.34% (12M), while leaders stay near 0.05–0.07% up to 3M. The same report notes that large systems "can use contextual information to create smaller candidate galleries," citing TVS. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202609/new-nist-frte-1n-results-illustrate-facial-recognition-accuracys-multidimensionality (16 Sep 2026). Academic work now trains directly for open-set metrics. An ECCV 2024 paper adds an identification-detection loss that cuts FNIR at 1% FPIR by 3–5% on average across face, gait and re-ID. [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16133 (Jul 2024); https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-73033-7_14. At flight scale (about 300–600 identities), exact search is trivial on CPU. ANN libraries such as FAISS (flat, IVF, HNSW, PQ indexes) matter only for airport-wide or national galleries. [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08281 (Douze et al., 2024); [I] flight-scale claim. Rules that exclude already-boarded passengers and require a margin between the best and second-best score are standard practice in theory, but no airline rule set is public. [I]

**P14 Resilience and fallback.** This is again industry practice. The circuit breaker (fail fast on an unhealthy dependency, then probe for recovery) is the canonical pattern [V] https://martinfowler.com/bliki/CircuitBreaker.html (6 Mar 2014) ⚠️OLD, and Resilience4j is its Java implementation, with circuit breakers, bulkheads, time limiters and retries. [V] https://resilience4j.readme.io/docs/circuitbreaker (undated). Aviation shows why degraded modes must be designed and rehearsed. The Collins Aerospace MUSE cyberattack (20–21 Sep 2025) forced Heathrow, Brussels, Berlin and Dublin to manual check-in and boarding. [V] https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-09-21/cyber-attack-collins-aerospace-disrupts-europe-airports/105798264 (21 Sep 2025). The EU EES reached full operation on 10 Apr 2026 [V] https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/entryexit-system-will-become-fully-operational-10-april-2026-2026-03-30_en (30 Mar 2026). By July 2026 queues reached up to 5 hours, and the Commission allowed temporary suspension of biometric collection at congested crossings until 6 Sep 2026; nine states asked to extend it. [V] https://time.com/article/2026/08/12/eu-europe-travel-entry-exit-system-ees-passport-biometric/ (12 Aug 2026). For the airline, the promise that passport and boarding pass stay "available at every touchpoint" (Phase 1) is the business-level fallback. How it is implemented technically (cached galleries, local match, manual lane) is not publicly known. [I]

**P15 Operations and model lifecycle.** Standard SRE practice monitors the four golden signals (latency, traffic, errors, saturation) against SLOs [V] https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/ (2016) ⚠️OLD. ML-specific production readiness adds monitoring for input drift, prediction distributions and model staleness. [V] https://research.google/pubs/the-ml-test-score-a-rubric-for-ml-production-readiness-and-technical-debt-reduction/ (Breck et al., 2017) ⚠️OLD. For face gates, true FNIR and FPIR are not observable live. Practical proxies are manual-fallback rate, retry count, genuine-score and top-2 margin distributions per gate and camera, and audited manual-check outcomes. [I] Matchers change often: NIST FRTE received 17 new or updated 1:1 submissions in one cycle [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202608/nist-frte-11-shows-facial-verification-accuracy-converging (11 Aug 2026). Templates are generally not comparable across algorithm versions, so an upgrade needs gallery re-extraction from source images, or a backward-compatible model. [I] Backward-compatible representation learning (BCT) trains a new embedding that stays comparable with the old gallery, avoiding re-indexing. [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942 (Shen et al., CVPR 2020) ⚠️OLD. It is used in retrieval research, and no public evidence shows face vendors use it. [I] In the airline's case the reference images sit with the identity authority, so who performs re-extraction is a key unknown. [I]

**P10 Consent enforcement and template protection (with P12, P19).** The operative guidance for EU passengers is still EDPB Opinion 11/2024. Template on the passenger's device (scenario 1), or centrally stored but encrypted with a passenger-held key (scenario 2), are "in principle compatible." Airport-controlled central 1:N (scenario 3.1) and airline-controlled cloud 1:N (scenario 4) "cannot be compatible with Article 25." [V] https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2024-05/edpb_opinion_202411_facialrecognitionairports_en.pdf (23 May 2024) ⚠️OLD but operative. ISO/IEC 24745:2022 covers "confidentiality, integrity and renewability/revocability during storage and transfer" of biometric information. [V] https://www.iso.org/standard/75302.html (2022) ⚠️OLD but current edition. Research evaluates protection schemes on irreversibility and unlinkability (see the April 2026 benchmark below). [V] Technique families:
- **Homomorphic encryption (HE).** IDFace (ICCV 2025) searches 1M encrypted templates in 126 ms at about 2× the unencrypted cost. [V] https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Kim_IDFace_Face_Template_Protection_for_Efficient_and_Secure_Identification_ICCV_2025_paper.html (2025)
- **Cancelable transforms** (BioHashing, IoM hashing, PolyProtect). An April 2026 benchmark on ArcFace templates found competitive accuracy (except Bloom filters) and near-unlinkability, but more leakage when the key is known. [V] https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13640-025-00679-y (2 Apr 2026). PolyProtect templates can be inverted unless keys are chosen carefully. [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03857 (May 2026)
- **Decentralised or passenger-held templates.** Examples are Digi Yatra and ICAO DTC (see P13).

Template-inversion attacks now reconstruct realistic faces from embeddings, including embeddings from privacy-preserving systems. [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13168 (Feb 2026; AAAI 2026). What none of these techniques address is the walk-through capture of people who have not consented. That is handled by engineering controls. The most documented is CBP's: partners purge images after transmission, and US citizens' photos are deleted within 12 hours. [V] Federal Register (27 Oct 2025). Restricting search to a consented gallery, keeping probes transient and in memory, and logging only pseudonymous decision records are INFERRED design choices, consistent with that practice.

**P4 Image quality.** ISO/IEC 29794-5 was published in April 2025, and a revision is already in progress. [V] https://www.iso.org/standard/81005.html; https://www.iso.org/standard/92472.html (AWI). Its reference implementation, OFIQ 1.0 (BSI, final report Nov 2024), assesses 20+ components such as lighting, head pose, sharpness and eye openness, and gives actionable feedback. [V] https://www.bsi.bund.de/EN/Themen/Unternehmen-und-Organisationen/Informationen-und-Empfehlungen/Freie-Software/OFIQ/OFIQ_node.html (accessed 29 Sep 2026). A mobile OFIQ 1.1 and an OFIQ 2 pre-release (Q4 2026) are planned. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/face-biometric-image-quality-tool-evolves-with-next-iso-standard (23 Jul 2026). Learned "utility" FIQA predicts recognition usefulness from the FR model itself:
- CR-FIQA (CVPR 2023) ⚠️OLD [V] https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Boutros_CR-FIQA_Face_Image_Quality_Assessment_by_Learning_Sample_Relative_Classifiability_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf
- CLIB-FIQA (CVPR 2024) ⚠️OLD [V] https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Ou_CLIB-FIQA_Face_Image_Quality_Assessment_with_Confidence_Calibration_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf
- ViT-FIQA (ICCVW 2025), which ranks among the top methods on EDC/pAUC benchmarks [V] https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13957v3 (2025)
- FROQ (training-free; Sep 2025) [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17689

Operational evidence is sobering. NIST FATE Quality found that the lowest-scored images are not consistently those producing false non-matches, and DHS called the OFIQ unified score of "extremely limited utility" for its use cases. [V] https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2026-01/26_0106_st_on_the_utility_of_the_open_source_facial_image_quality_tool_for_facial_biometric_recognition_in_dhs.pdf (Jan 2026); https://www.biometricupdate.com/202609/better-face-recognition-exposes-limits-of-image-quality-scores (24 Sep 2026). The current consensus is to use quality for capture feedback and best-shot selection rather than hard rejection. [V] same source.

**P6 Presentation attack detection.** Testing is standardised in ISO/IEC 30107-3:2023 (APCER/BPCER). [V] https://www.iso.org/standard/79520.html (2023) ⚠️OLD but current edition. Commercial PAD is certified through iBeta Level 1 (0% penetration allowed), Level 2 (1%) and Level 3 (5%), with BPCER capped at 15% (10% at Level 3). Letters have been issued as recently as 17 Sep 2026. [V] https://www.ibeta.com/iso-30107-3-presentation-attack-detection-confirmation-letters/ (accessed 29 Sep 2026). NIST's passive-PAD evaluation found accuracy "varies widely" (Phase 1). [V] https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8491.pdf (Sep 2023) ⚠️OLD. Approach families:
- **Passive single-frame RGB with pixel-wise supervision** (CDCN, CVPR 2020) ⚠️OLD [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04092
- **Domain-generalised, vision-language approaches** for unseen attacks: FLIP (ICCV 2023) ⚠️OLD, CFPL-FAS (CVPR 2024) ⚠️OLD, and 2025 MLLM reinforcement fine-tuning [V] https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Srivatsan_FLIP_Cross-domain_Face_Anti-spoofing_with_Language_Guidance_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Liu_CFPL-FAS_Class_Free_Prompt_Learning_for_Generalizable_Face_Anti-spoofing_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf; https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21895 (Jun 2025)
- **Unified physical and digital attack detection**, the ICCV 2025 challenge theme [V] https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025W/FAS2025/html/Ma_UniAttackData_Unified_Physical-Digital_Attack_Detection_Challenge_ICCVW_2025_paper.html (2025)
- **Multi-spectral hardware PAD** (RGB, NIR, depth, thermal) [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08848 (George et al., TIFS 2020) ⚠️OLD

Challenge-based ("active") liveness adds seconds of user interaction and does not suit walk-through gates. [I] It fits app enrolment, where injection attacks (virtual cameras, deepfakes) are the main threat. CEN/TS 18099 (approved Oct 2024) defines injection-attack detection testing, and ISO/IEC 25456 is under development. [V] https://withpersona.com/blog/what-is-cen-ts-18099-a-guide-to-the-injection-attack-detection-standard/ (1 Jul 2026; secondary)

**P7 Demographic differentials.** ISO/IEC 19795-10:2024 standardises how to measure false-negative and false-positive differentials and an aggregate equitability measure, with uncertainty. [V] https://www.iso.org/standard/81223.html; https://www.biometricupdate.com/202410/publication-of-iso-standard-sets-up-biometric-bias-tests-and-measurement (11 Oct 2024). NIST's September 2026 FRTE 1:1 update still finds West African subjects the highest-disparity regional group for 97.3% of algorithms and females for 98.8%, with a median Bias Gini of 0.62. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202609/nist-frte-11-update-shows-shifting-leaders-persistent-demographic-disparities (21 Sep 2026). Acquisition is also a source of bias: in the DHS 2022 rally, match rates for darker skin tones in groups of 4 fell to 88.8%. [V] https://www.biometricupdate.com/202302/dhs-biometric-rally-results-show-groups-pose-little-challenge-to-effective-matching (23 Feb 2023) ⚠️OLD. Post-hoc score calibration without sensitive labels (FairCal) is the main research mitigation that avoids retraining. [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03761 (ICLR 2022) ⚠️OLD

**P2/P16 Walk-through, video, multi-face and tailgating.** NIST FIVE 2024 reports the self-boarding-gate miss rate falling from 0.09 (2015) to 0.00 (2024), with 31 algorithms from 11 developers. Harder video (concourse, long range) still has misses. [V] https://pages.nist.gov/ifpc/2025/presentations/03.pdf (1 Apr 2025). The DHS 2022 rally (groups of 2–12 walking through) found the best acquisition-plus-matching combination reached 97.4% true identification, and 97% of errors came from acquisition. [V] (23 Feb 2023) ⚠️OLD. The typical pipeline is:
1. Efficient multi-face detection: SCRFD (ICLR 2022) ⚠️OLD [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04714
2. Tracking so each person yields one decision: ByteTrack (ECCV 2022) ⚠️OLD [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06864
3. Combining frames into one template: learned aggregation such as CAFace (NeurIPS 2022) ⚠️OLD [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10864, or simpler quality-weighted best-shot selection [I]

Tailgating detection is thin academically (a 2019 body-feet piggybacking paper; AIT industrial work). [V] titles only: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-33904-3_74 (not read, rate-limited); https://www.ait.ac.at/en/research-topics/surveillance-protection/video-analytics/tailgating-and-piggybacking-detection. In production it is usually solved with physical sensing (mantrap, overhead depth) plus a "single face present" check, which OFIQ includes (Phase 1). [I]

**Caveats (benchmarks vs real gates):**
1. FRTE uses high-quality visa, border and mugshot images at fixed operating points. Gate probes come from a walk-through camera at about 1 m, where acquisition dominates errors. [V] Rally; [I] mapping.
2. FRTE 1:N galleries hold millions of identities, while a flight gallery holds hundreds. Flight galleries give a much lower background false-positive rate, but look-alikes, twins and families sit in the same small gallery. [V] Phase 1, NIST IR 8439 ⚠️OLD; [I]
3. Accuracy leaderboards omit end-to-end latency (capture, DCS lookup, identity-authority call, gate actuation). FRTE reports timing but not system SLAs. [V] NIST page; [I]
4. The Paperless Travel leaderboard was last updated in March 2024 ⚠️OLD and is the most gate-relevant NIST view.
5. PAD certification is lab-based with fixed attack species. Field attack rates and injection at app enrolment are separate problems. [I]

## D. Gaps between research results and airport reality

1. **Acquisition, not matching, is the bottleneck.** Academic benchmarks start from cropped faces, but at walk-through gates 97% of errors came from acquisition. [V] (Rally 2022 ⚠️OLD). A POC should measure capture-to-decision, not just match accuracy. [I]
2. **Operating points differ by orders of magnitude.** IJB-C reports at FAR 1e-4. LVFace-B drops from 97.70% to 90.06% TAR when FAR moves from 1e-4 to 1e-6. [V] Gate thresholds are set on FPIR over the flight gallery, not on FAR. [I]
3. **Gallery design is business logic, not an ML problem.** Research studies closed or open-set search. Gates need manifest sync, boarded-state exclusion, top-2 margin rules and re-presentation handling, and no public paper covers this. [I]
4. **Quality scores ≠ recognition failure.** Rejecting on UQS would discard many usable images (UNHCR ~90% usable). [V] Research FIQA is benchmarked with EDC curves, not with operational capture UX. [I]
5. **PAD generalisation.** Academic FAS still struggles across domains [V survey], and certified PAD is tested against lab attack species. Injection attacks target app enrolment, not gates. [V/I]
6. **Demographics and population mix.** Disparities persist (NIST, Sep 2026) [V]. Public fairness benchmarks do not match DXB's passenger mix (India, Saudi Arabia, UK, Pakistan per Phase 1). The airline's monitoring approach is not publicly known. [I]
7. **Template protection vs government-owned identity.** Research protects templates the operator owns. At the airline, the reference sits with the identity authority, and the airline may receive only a decision, which would change what the airline must protect. The contract is not publicly known. [I] Template inversion (AAAI 2026) means stored embeddings must be treated as biometric data. [V]
8. **Consent is not a crypto problem.** HE and cancelable schemes do not prevent a walk-through camera from detecting people who have not consented. Engineering controls (gallery restriction, transient probes, purge, audit) do. [I] CBP's purge rules are the documented precedent. [V]
9. **Model lifecycle.** Research rarely addresses upgrades. FRTE shows constant resubmission [V], and re-templating a gallery owned by a third party (the identity authority) is an operational dependency. [I] BCT exists [V] but has no public evidence of use in face gates. [I]
10. **Licensing.** Most open weights (InsightFace, LVFace) are non-commercial. They are fine for a POC, but production uses licensed SDKs. [V] licences; [I] implication.
11. **Degraded modes at scale.** EES and the Collins MUSE incident show systems must fall back without stopping flows [V]. No research benchmark covers fallback behaviour. [I]

## E. New open questions (from Phase 2)
1. Does the gate receive only a match decision from the identity authority (TVS-style), or scores and templates that the airline thresholds itself? This decides who owns P1 tuning. *(Not publicly known.)*
2. Which NIST FRTE category best represents your gate images (visa–border, kiosk–border), and does the team track a per-gate proxy such as manual-fallback rate?
3. What PAD assurance do you require from vendors (for example iBeta ISO/IEC 30107-3 Level 2), and is there injection-attack testing for app enrolment?
4. When the matcher is upgraded, who re-extracts templates from reference images, the airline, the vendor or the identity authority, and how is the rollout canaried?
5. Is image quality used as capture feedback at the gate, or as a hard reject?
